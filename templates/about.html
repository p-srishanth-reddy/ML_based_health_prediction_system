AI-Based Early Prediction of Health Diseases

An academic clinical analytics demonstration illustrating machine learning pipelines for condition-specific risk screening, model explainability, and comparative evaluation.

Condition-Specific Architecture

Many naive healthcare demonstrations attempt to force disparate health conditions into a single generic input form. In contrast, this project maintains four isolated, condition-specific modules, each with its own independent clinical dataset, target label definition, validated schema, preprocessing pipeline, and model artifacts:

Hypertension / Blood Pressure

Evaluates physiological and familial risk using age, BMI, hemoglobin concentration, genetic pedigree coefficient, and chronic renal history.

Diabetes / Blood Sugar

Screens for Type-2 diabetes risk based on fasting glucose concentration, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), patient age, and body mass index.

Anemia Risk

Hematology screening based on biological sex, hemoglobin levels, and red cell volume indices (MCH, MCHC, MCV).

Obesity & Body Mass

Classifies 7 distinct body mass tiers (Underweight, Normal, Overweight Levels I-II, Obesity Types I-III) based on anthropometrics and dietary habits.

Data Leakage Prevention & Rigorous Validation

Previous iterations of the anemia and diabetes datasets contained duplicated records that caused severe test-set leakage when randomly partitioned, inflating accuracy to fictitious 100% metrics. In our improved pipeline:

  • Dataset Deduplication: Duplicate rows are purged before splitting, guaranteeing zero identical sample overlap between training and testing.
  • Stratified Holdout Split: A clean 20% test partition is set aside with class proportions preserved.
  • 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation: Preprocessing pipelines (imputation, standard scaling, and one-hot encoding) are fitted strictly within folds to prevent feature leakage.
  • Comprehensive Metric Reporting: In addition to accuracy, models are evaluated on Balanced Accuracy, Precision, Recall/Sensitivity, Specificity, F1-Score, and ROC-AUC / PR-AUC.
Explainability: Feature Association vs. Causation

When screening a patient, the system extracts global feature importance weights and computes local patient-level deviations relative to clinical reference baselines. Importantly, statistical association is NOT biological causation: while elevated glucose strongly predicts diabetes risk in dataset records, the model does not assert direct medical causation.
